================================================================================
PART 1 — STARTING POINT AND ENGINEERING OBJECTIVE
================================================================================

The objective was not simply to make a graph that says "4000 ft".

The engineering objective was to build a model-based flight-control chain:

    Requirements
        |
        v
    Aircraft / plant model
        |
        v
    Trim operating point
        |
        v
    Linearization
        |
        v
    State-space model
        |
        v
    Discrete controller design
        |
        v
    Simulink implementation
        |
        v
    JSBSim nonlinear plant
        |
        v
    Altitude response
        |
        v
    Verification

The target operating condition was:

    altitude       ~= 4000 ft
    forward speed ~= 185 ft/s
    gamma          ~= 0 deg

The reason for selecting a trim point is fundamental:

    LQR is a local linear controller.

An aircraft is nonlinear:

    x_dot = f(x,u)

Around an equilibrium point:

    x0, u0

the nonlinear model can be approximated by:

    delta_x_dot = A delta_x + B delta_u

where:

    delta_x = x - x0
    delta_u = u - u0

Therefore the controller must work with deviations from the operating point,
not arbitrary absolute aircraft values.

================================================================================
PART 2 — WHY A SIMPLE ALTITUDE PID WAS NOT THE FINAL ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

A first instinct is:

    altitude error
        |
        v
       PID
        |
        v
     elevator

That is insufficient as the complete aircraft architecture.

Altitude and airspeed are coupled.

If the elevator is used to pitch the aircraft upward:

    pitch up
       |
       v
    altitude increases
       |
       v
    kinetic energy is consumed
       |
       v
    airspeed decreases

If airspeed becomes too low:

    lift / dynamic response changes
       |
       v
    the aircraft may lose the ability to maintain altitude

Therefore the final architecture separates the main responsibilities:

    Elevator:
        pitch / flight-path response

    Throttle:
        airspeed / energy support

    Aileron:
        bank stabilization using a PI wing-leveler

    Rudder:
        currently held at zero in the implemented model

The altitude loop therefore commands a pitch objective, while the inner
controller stabilizes the aircraft about that pitch objective.

================================================================================
PART 3 — WHY LINEARIZATION WAS NECESSARY
================================================================================

LQR requires a linear state-space model:

    x[k+1] = A_d x[k] + B_d u[k]

The real JSBSim plant is nonlinear.

Therefore the workflow became:

    1. Trim JSBSim.
    2. Verify the trim.
    3. Perturb elevator.
    4. Extract local dynamics.
    5. Construct A and B.
    6. Discretize A and B.
    7. Design DLQR.
    8. Verify the linear closed loop.
    9. Only then put the controller into nonlinear Simulink.

This separation was one of the most important lessons of the project.

If the linear controller fails:
    fix the mathematical/controller implementation.

If the linear controller passes but nonlinear JSBSim fails:
    investigate nonlinear implementation, trim offsets, actuator conventions,
    operating range, saturation, missing loops, or model limitations.

Do NOT randomly change A, B, K, XML, and Simulink at the same time.

PART 5 — WHY THE AIRCRAFT CAN DIVERGE WHEN K = 0
================================================================================

One of the useful tests was to remove active DLQR control and observe the
plant.

The successful native trim proves that an equilibrium exists.

It does NOT prove that the aircraft will remain exactly at that equilibrium
for an arbitrarily long nonlinear simulation when the active feedback loop
is removed.

The zero-elevator/free-flight diagnostic showed approximately:

    t = 0 s:
        h ~= 4000 ft

    t = 5 s:
        h ~= 4002.46 ft

    t = 10 s:
        h ~= 3944.26 ft

    t = 15 s:
        h ~= 3868.67 ft

Therefore:

    open-loop/free-flight departure from trim is real.

This is not automatically evidence of a bug in JSBSim.

An aircraft requires active feedback to reject disturbances and maintain
commanded states.

This was an important conceptual correction during the project.

PART 8 — FOUR-STATE LONGITUDINAL MODEL
================================================================================

The final controller state vector was:

    x =
        [ delta_u
          delta_alpha
          delta_theta
          q ]

The exact state order matters.

It is:

    STATE 1:
        delta_u

    STATE 2:
        delta_alpha

    STATE 3:
        delta_theta

    STATE 4:
        q

A major source of wasted time earlier was uncertainty about whether the third
and fourth Mux entries should be:

    q, theta

or:

    theta, q

The verified final order is:

    delta_theta THIRD
    q FOURTH

The final Simulink model must preserve this order.

Definitions:

    delta_u =
        u_actual - u_trim

    delta_alpha =
        alpha_actual - alpha_trim

    delta_theta =
        theta_actual - theta_command

    q =
        actual pitch rate

This is a perturbation controller.

It does not directly use:

    [u, alpha, theta, q]

as the LQR vector.

It uses deviations from the selected operating point/reference.

================================================================================
PART 11 — DLQR DESIGN
================================================================================

The controller minimizes a discrete quadratic cost of the form:

    J = sum [
        x_k' Q x_k
        +
        u_k' R u_k
    ]

The design uses:

    u_k = -K x_k

The selected Bryson-style weights were:

    Q =
        diag(
            0.0100,
            69.4444,
            400.0000,
            44.4444
        )

    R =
        6.2500

The resulting gain was:

    K =
        [ 0.0205
          2.2685
         -7.4923
         -2.1182 ]

Therefore:

    delta_e = -K*x

or explicitly:

    delta_e =
        -(0.0205*delta_u
          + 2.2685*delta_alpha
          - 7.4923*delta_theta
          - 2.1182*q)

The minus sign belongs to the control law.

Therefore do not accidentally implement:

    delta_e = K*x

when the designed law is:

    delta_e = -K*x

The Simulink implementation uses a Gain block containing K followed by a
Gain block of -1, which is mathematically equivalent.

================================================================================
PART 13 — WHY THE LINEAR TEST AND NONLINEAR JSBSIM TEST DIFFER
================================================================================

This distinction is fundamental.

The linear test assumes:

    x[k+1] = Ad*x[k] + Bd*u[k]

around one trim point.

The nonlinear test runs:

    x_dot = f(x,u)

through the full C172P JSBSim model.

As the aircraft moves farther from trim:

    nonlinear effects become larger

    actuator nonlinearities become relevant

    saturation becomes relevant

    aerodynamic coefficients vary

    throttle/elevator coupling changes

    other aircraft states may become important

    lateral-directional effects may appear

Therefore:

    stable linear model

does not imply:

    globally stable nonlinear aircraft.

This was one of the main reasons the project initially appeared to be
"going in circles."

The correct engineering workflow is to use the linear model to verify the
controller first, then progressively validate the nonlinear implementation.

PART 14 — TRIM OFFSET AND BASELINE CONTROL
================================================================================

A critical implementation principle was:

    ACTUAL ACTUATOR COMMAND
        =
    TRIM COMMAND
        +
    CONTROLLER PERTURBATION

For elevator:

    elevator_command =
        elevator_trim + delta_e

The trim value used in the Simulink model is:

    elevator trim ~= 0.2021 normalized

The controller output is the perturbation.

Therefore the DLQR should not be interpreted as generating the entire
absolute elevator command from zero.

Similarly for throttle:

    throttle_command =
        throttle_trim + throttle_perturbation

with:

    throttle_trim ~= 0.736296

This distinction is essential because the C172P's physical elevator is not
zero at the selected trimmed operating point.

================================================================================
PART 28 — BANK / LATERAL STABILIZATION — FINAL PI IMPLEMENTATION
================================================================================

The final model contains a bank stabilization loop implemented as a
proportional-integral (PI) feedback controller.

This section supersedes the earlier proportional-only implementation.

The evolution of this loop is important because the proportional-only version
solved one problem but exposed a second, smaller steady-state problem.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.1 — WHY THE AILERON LOOP WAS ADDED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Initially, the aileron input was effectively zero.

With no lateral stabilization, the nonlinear C172P simulation could develop
uncontrolled roll/bank behavior. The aircraft could enter increasingly
undesirable lateral motion, lose altitude, and eventually become unstable.

Therefore a simple bank-feedback loop was introduced before attempting a more
complete lateral-directional controller.

The immediate requirement was:

    prevent uncontrolled bank
        |
        v
    prevent lateral motion from becoming a major source of altitude loss.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.2 — FIRST WORKING VERSION: PROPORTIONAL WING-LEVELER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first successful aileron feedback law was:

    aileron_command =
        sat(-0.5 * phi)

where:

    phi = aircraft bank angle in radians

This is a proportional controller:

    delta_a = -K_phi * phi

with:

    K_phi = 0.5

The signal path was:

    phi
      |
      v
    Gain(-0.5)
      |
      v
    Aileron command
      |
      v
    Saturation [-1,+1]
      |
      v
    JSBSim input 2

This successfully prevented the severe uncontrolled roll behavior.

However, the proportional controller did not remove the steady-state bank
error completely.

The aircraft eventually settled approximately at:

    phi ~= -0.032 rad

which is approximately:

    phi ~= -1.8 deg

At the same time:

    roll rate p -> 0

Therefore the aircraft was no longer continuously rolling. It had stabilized
at a small nonzero bank angle.

Because a nonzero bank angle creates a horizontal component of lift, the
aircraft entered a slow coordinated turn.

The measured behavior was approximately:

    phi  ~= -0.032 rad
    p    ~= 0 rad/s
    beta ~= approximately 0 rad
    r    ~= -0.006 rad/s

The sideslip being approximately zero showed that this was not primarily a
rudder/sideslip problem.

The yaw rate was consistent with a shallow coordinated turn produced by the
persistent bank angle.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.3 — WHY THE PROPORTIONAL CONTROLLER LEFT A STEADY BANK ERROR
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proportional controller was:

    delta_a = -K_phi * phi

A pure proportional controller can leave a steady-state error when the plant
requires a nonzero steady control input to maintain the desired equilibrium.

In this case, suppose the aircraft requires some nonzero aileron bias to remain
wings-level because of the nonlinear aircraft dynamics and lateral coupling.

For the proportional controller to generate that nonzero aileron command, it
must have:

    phi != 0

because:

    delta_a = -K_phi * phi

Therefore the controller can settle at:

    phi_ss != 0

while still producing:

    delta_a_ss != 0

This explains the observed condition:

    persistent bank
        |
        v
    horizontal lift component
        |
        v
    nonzero yaw rate
        |
        v
    continuously changing heading
        |
        v
    aircraft slowly circles.

The proportional loop had therefore solved the original instability problem
but still had a steady-state regulation error.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.4 — DIAGNOSTIC MEASUREMENTS USED BEFORE CHANGING THE CONTROLLER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The lateral behavior was diagnosed using four important signals:

    1. Aileron command
    2. Bank angle phi
    3. Sideslip beta
    4. Yaw rate r

A roll-rate measurement p was then added to distinguish:

    "the aircraft is still rolling"

from:

    "the aircraft has stopped rolling but remains banked."

The observed results were:

    Aileron:
        settled around a small nonzero value

    phi:
        settled around -0.032 rad

    beta:
        converged close to zero

    r:
        settled around -0.006 rad/s

    p:
        converged essentially to zero

This established the actual physical situation:

    p ~= 0
        means no continuing roll motion

    phi != 0
        means the aircraft remains banked

    beta ~= 0
        means the aircraft is approximately coordinated

    r != 0
        means the aircraft continues turning

Therefore adding an arbitrary constant rudder command would not have addressed
the root problem.

The required correction was to eliminate the steady-state bank-angle error.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.5 — FINAL PI WING-LEVELER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proportional wing-leveler was upgraded to a PI controller.

The final tested control law is:

    delta_a =
        sat(
            -0.5 * phi
            -0.01 * integral(phi)
        )

or equivalently:

    delta_a =
        sat(
            -K_p * phi
            -K_i * integral(phi)
        )

with:

    K_p = 0.5

    K_i = 0.01

The integral term is the accumulated bank-angle error:

    integral(phi)
        =
    integral from 0 to t of phi(t) dt

Therefore the complete controller is:

    phi
     |
     +----------------------+
     |                      |
     v                      v
    Gain -0.5          Integrator
     |                      |
     |                   Gain -0.01
     |                      |
     +----------+-----------+
                |
                v
          Aileron command
                |
                v
          Saturation [-1,+1]
                |
                v
             JSBSim
                |
                v
           C172P aircraft
                |
                v
                phi
                |
                +------ feedback

The negative signs are intentional.

If the aircraft develops a positive bank error:

    phi > 0

the proportional contribution is negative:

    -0.5 * phi < 0

and the integral contribution accumulates in the corrective direction.

If a small bank error persists, the integral term continues to build the
required corrective aileron bias until the steady-state bank error is removed.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.6 — WHY THE INTEGRAL TERM SOLVES THE OBSERVED PROBLEM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The key difference is:

PROPORTIONAL ONLY:

    delta_a = -K_p * phi

A persistent nonzero aileron requirement forces:

    phi_ss != 0

PI:

    delta_a =
        -K_p * phi
        -K_i * integral(phi)

Now the controller can provide a nonzero steady aileron contribution through
the integral state even when:

    phi -> 0

Therefore the aircraft no longer needs to remain banked simply to generate
the corrective aileron command.

The desired steady condition becomes:

    phi -> 0

while the final aileron command is allowed to settle at whatever small value
is required by the nonlinear plant.

This is an important controls principle:

    The objective is not necessarily:
        aileron -> 0

    The objective is:
        bank-angle error -> 0

A nonzero steady actuator command can be perfectly valid if it is required to
maintain the desired equilibrium.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The PI modification was tested in the nonlinear JSBSim + Simulink model.

The aircraft changed from:

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.8 — WHY RUDDER WAS NOT ADDED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A rudder feedback controller was initially considered because the aircraft was
visibly turning.

However, the measured sideslip was already approximately zero.

Therefore:

    beta ~= 0

while:

    phi != 0
    r != 0

This showed that the aircraft was approximately coordinated but banked.

The root problem was therefore not primarily:

    excessive sideslip

but:

    persistent bank-angle error.

Consequently, the final implementation keeps:

    rudder_command = 0

and uses the PI aileron loop to regulate bank angle.

This is deliberately a limited lateral stabilization layer rather than a full
lateral-directional autopilot.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.9 — FINAL IMPLEMENTED RELATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The final bank-control relation is:

    aileron_command =
        sat(
            -0.5 * phi
            -0.01 * integral(phi),
            -1,
            +1
        )

where:

    phi:
        bank angle in radians

    0.5:
        proportional bank-feedback gain

    0.01:
        integral bank-feedback gain

    integral(phi):
        accumulated bank-angle error

    [-1,+1]:
        final aileron command saturation.

This is the final implementation that produced straight flight in the
nonlinear simulation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.10 — CONTROL-SYSTEM INTERPRETATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The PI wing-leveler should be described as:

    a lateral bank-stabilization / wings-level control loop.

It should NOT be described as:

    a full lateral-directional autopilot.

The current controller does not explicitly command or regulate:

    heading psi

and it does not implement a full coordinated-turn/heading controller.

Its responsibility is narrower:

    regulate phi toward zero.

This is appropriate for the current altitude-hold demonstration because the
main objective is to maintain altitude while preventing lateral bank from
becoming an additional source of altitude error.

================================================================================
PART 32 — WHAT CAUSED THE EARLY CONFUSION
================================================================================

Several problems were not failures of control theory; they were interface and
model-definition ambiguities.

1. STATE ORDER CONFUSION
------------------------

Earlier configurations alternated between:

    [du, da, q, theta]

and:

    [du, da, theta, q]

The final verified controller requires:

    [du, da, dtheta, q]

This matters because K is not interchangeable under a state permutation.

For example:

    K3 = -7.4923

belongs to:

    delta_theta

and:

    K4 = -2.1182

belongs to:

    q

Swapping them changes the controller.

2. ABSOLUTE VS PERTURBATION STATES
-----------------------------------

Using:

    theta

instead of:

    theta - theta_cmd

would cause the inner controller to regulate the wrong quantity.

Likewise:

    U

instead of:

    U - U_trim

would be inconsistent with the linearized model.

3. TRIM ELEVATOR CONFUSION
---------------------------

The command:

    elevator-cmd-norm = 0

was initially easy to misinterpret.

The aircraft nevertheless had:

    elevator-pos-rad ~= 0.0811 rad

because pitch trim was active.

Therefore the correct architecture must distinguish:

    trim command

from:

    controller perturbation.

4. STARTING IN MID-AIR
----------------------

Starting at:

    4000 ft

does not itself make the simulation wrong.

It is intentional when testing a trim-point controller.

The purpose is to start close to the equilibrium and evaluate regulation.

A takeoff simulation would answer a different question.

5. OPEN-LOOP DIVERGENCE
-----------------------

Seeing altitude fall with:

    K = 0

does not prove that the model is broken.

It demonstrates that active feedback is required.

6. LINEAR VS NONLINEAR RESULTS
------------------------------

The linear model was stable.

The nonlinear JSBSim result showed larger deviations.

This does not contradict the linear result.

The linear model is local.

7. XML VERSION PROLIFERATION
-----------------------------

Many diagnostic XML variants were created during troubleshooting.

This became a major source of confusion.

The solution is to separate:

    diagnostic XML

from:

    final simulation XML

and never silently switch between them.

PART 41 — HOW TO DIAGNOSE A FUTURE ALTITUDE FAILURE
================================================================================

If altitude falls:

DO NOT immediately change K.

First ask:

    1. Is theta_cmd changing?
    2. Is theta_actual following theta_cmd?
    3. Is q responding correctly?
    4. Is elevator command changing?
    5. Is physical elevator position changing?
    6. Is U falling?
    7. Is throttle increasing?
    8. Is phi becoming large?
    9. Is the elevator saturated?
   10. Is the aircraft leaving the linearization region?

Interpretation:

If:

    theta_cmd changes

but:

    elevator does not change

then the inner-loop wiring is wrong.

If:

    elevator changes

but:

    theta does not respond

then investigate actuator sign, trim, JSBSim input mapping, or plant behavior.

If:

    theta tracks

but:

    altitude still falls

then investigate:

    flight-path angle
    airspeed
    vertical velocity
    energy management.

If:

    U falls severely

then the altitude loop may be demanding too much pitch without enough
throttle response.

If:

    phi grows

then lateral-directional behavior may be contributing to altitude loss.

If:

    all inner-loop states remain small

but nonlinear altitude drifts slowly,

then the issue may be outer-loop tuning, trim mismatch, or nonlinear
steady-state error.

    approximate aircraft locally around equilibrium

    DLQR
        =
    stabilize the four longitudinal perturbation states

    Outer altitude PID
        =
    tell the pitch loop what pitch perturbation is needed to correct altitude

    Speed PID
        =
    manage kinetic energy / throttle

    Bank PI loop
        =
    drive bank angle toward zero and remove the steady-state bank error that
    previously caused a slow coordinated turn

    Simulink
        =
    controller implementation and signal routing

    JSBSim S-Function
        =
    nonlinear plant interface

The final loop is therefore:

    altitude error

PART 48 — CURRENT LATERAL-CONTROL STATUS AFTER PI UPDATE
================================================================================

The lateral-control portion of the project has now progressed through three
states:

    STATE 1 — NO AILERON FEEDBACK
        aileron = 0

        Result:
            uncontrolled lateral/roll behavior could develop, leading to
            altitude loss and eventual instability.

    STATE 2 — PROPORTIONAL WING-LEVELER
        aileron = sat(-0.5*phi, -1, +1)

        Result:
            roll behavior became stable, but the aircraft settled at
            approximately:

                phi ~= -0.032 rad

            with approximately:

                beta ~= 0
                p ~= 0
                r ~= -0.006 rad/s

            This produced a slow coordinated turn.

    STATE 3 — FINAL PI WING-LEVELER
        aileron =
            sat(-0.5*phi - 0.01*integral(phi), -1, +1)

        Result:
            the steady-state bank error was removed and the aircraft now flies
            approximately straight instead of continuously circling.

The final interpretation is:

    P term:
        provides immediate bank stabilization.

    I term:
        removes persistent bank-angle error by accumulating the error and
        generating the required steady corrective aileron bias.

The final PI loop is therefore part of the current validated nonlinear
altitude-hold architecture.

The current lateral-control scope remains intentionally limited:

    regulated variable:
        bank angle phi

    actuator:
        aileron

    rudder:
        fixed at zero

    heading:
        not explicitly controlled

This should be described accurately in future portfolio documentation.
